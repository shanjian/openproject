# Company OKR Framework

## Purpose

Objectives and Key Results (OKRs) are our framework for aligning strategy, execution, and accountability across the organization.

OKRs help us:

- Focus on the most important priorities
- Align teams around common goals
- Measure outcomes rather than activities
- Increase transparency
- Connect daily work to company strategy

For instructions on how to create and manage OKRs in OpenProject, see:

**Department and Team OKR Process**

# OKR Structure

Every OKR consists of:

```text
Strategic Initiative
└── Objective
    └── Key Results
```

Supporting Tasks may be created under Key Results when needed.

### Strategic Initiative

A long-term strategic priority.

Examples:

- Subscription Growth
- Audience Growth
- Product Excellence
- Operational Excellence

Strategic Initiatives provide direction.

### Objective

An Objective describes what we want to achieve.

Objectives should be:

- Ambitious
- Clear
- Outcome-oriented
- Time-bound

Good examples:

- Increase subscriber retention
- Improve reader engagement
- Strengthen platform reliability

Poor examples:

- Launch a newsletter
- Build a dashboard
- Hire developers

Objectives describe outcomes, not activities.

### Key Result

A Key Result defines how success is measured.

Key Results should be:

- Specific
- Measurable
- Time-bound
- Verifiable

Good examples:

- Increase annual renewals from 65% to 75%
- Increase newsletter open rate from 30% to 40%
- Reduce page load time by 30%

Poor examples:

- Improve newsletter performance
- Redesign the homepage
- Create a dashboard

Key Results measure outcomes, not effort.

# Ownership

Every Objective and Key Result must have one owner.

In OpenProject, the **Accountable** field is used to identify the owner.

Contributors may assist.

Accountability belongs to a single person.

Good:

```text
Accountable: Director of Growth
```

Poor:

```text
Accountable: Marketing + Product + Engineering
```

Shared ownership often results in unclear accountability.

# Organizational Ownership

Every Objective and Key Result must be assigned to an **Organizational Unit**.

The Organizational Unit identifies which part of the organization owns the OKR.

Examples:

```text
Company
Marketing
Marketing / Growth
Product
Product / Platform
Editorial
Technology
```

The Organizational Unit is used for filtering, reporting, and department or team reviews.

Ownership and Organizational Unit are different:

```text
Organizational Unit: Marketing / Growth
Accountable: Director of Growth
```

The Organizational Unit identifies the responsible organization.

The Accountable field identifies the person responsible for the result.

# Measuring Success

Success is measured by outcomes, not task completion.

Questions we ask:

- Did we achieve the intended result?
- Did the metric improve?
- Did the organization move closer to its strategic goals?

We do not measure success by:

- Number of meetings
- Number of tasks completed
- Amount of effort invested

Completing all Tasks under a Key Result does not automatically mean the Key Result has been achieved.

The Key Result is measured against its defined metric and target.

# OKR Scoring

At the end of each quarter, Key Results are scored based on actual results.

### Target Range

For ambitious OKRs, the ideal score is typically:

**0.7–0.8**

Consistently achieving 1.0 may indicate goals are not ambitious enough.

The score should reflect achievement of the Key Result itself, not completion of its supporting Tasks.

# OKR Health

OKR Health indicates whether an Objective or Key Result is expected to achieve its intended outcome.

Use:

- **Not Started** — meaningful work has not yet begun
- **On Track** — expected to achieve the target.
- **At Risk** — target is still achievable, but attention or intervention is needed
- **Off Track** — target is unlikely to be achieved without a significant change

OKR Health is different from OpenProject workflow status. The workflow status
describes the lifecycle of the OKR item (Draft → Committed →
Completed / Moved to Next Quarter — see **Department and Team OKR Process**),
while OKR Health describes the likelihood of achieving the intended result.

For example:

```text
Status: Committed
OKR Health: At Risk
```

# Guiding Principles

## Focus

A small number of important goals is better than many competing priorities.

Recommended:

- 3–5 Objectives per department per quarter
- 2–5 Key Results per Objective

## Transparency

OKRs are visible across the organization.

Teams should understand:

- What other teams are trying to achieve
- How their work contributes to company goals
- Where dependencies exist

## Alignment

Every Key Result should support an Objective.

Every Objective should support a Strategic Initiative.

Supporting Tasks should contribute to a Key Result.

No work should exist without a strategic purpose.

## Continuous Improvement

OKRs are not performance reviews.

They are a tool for learning, prioritization, and continuous improvement.

Teams are encouraged to:

- Take informed risks
- Set ambitious goals
- Learn from outcomes
- Adjust when necessary
